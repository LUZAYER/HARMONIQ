# HARMONIQ — Research & Engineering Skill

## 1. Project Identity

**Framework Name:** HARMONIQ

**Full Name:** Hybrid Assessment and Reliability Model for Output Natural-language Integrity and Quality

**Research Title:** HARMONIQ: A Hybrid Multi-Signal Framework for Hallucination Detection in Large Language Models

**Research Domain:** Natural Language Processing (NLP), Large Language Models, AI Reliability, Factuality Assessment, Hallucination Detection, and Evidence-Based Verification.

HARMONIQ is a research-oriented hybrid framework designed to identify, analyze, and assess hallucinations in responses generated by Large Language Models (LLMs).

The framework combines multiple complementary detection signals to investigate whether their integration can improve hallucination-detection reliability compared with individual detection approaches.

**Important:** HARMONIQ is a proposed research framework. Never present its effectiveness, accuracy, or superiority as established until supported by reproducible experimental results.

---

## 2. Core Research Objective

The primary objective of HARMONIQ is to detect unsupported, fabricated, contradictory, or factually inaccurate information generated by LLMs.

Given a user query and an LLM-generated response, the framework should:

1. Decompose the response into independently verifiable factual claims.
2. Analyze each claim using multiple detection techniques.
3. Retrieve relevant external evidence where available.
4. Evaluate semantic consistency between claims and evidence.
5. Perform independent model-based verification.
6. Combine detection signals through a decision-fusion mechanism.
7. Calculate claim-level and response-level hallucination scores.
8. Identify potentially hallucinated claims.
9. Provide evidence-based explanations for its decisions.

### Central Research Question

Can complementary hallucination-detection signals be combined into a lightweight and explainable framework that detects LLM hallucinations more reliably than individual detection methods?

### Research Hypothesis

A hybrid framework combining confidence analysis, retrieval-based verification, semantic consistency analysis, and self-verification may achieve more reliable hallucination detection than individual detection approaches while providing interpretable evidence.

This hypothesis must be tested experimentally.

---

## 3. Fundamental Research Principles

Every implementation and research decision must follow these principles:

### 3.1 Evidence Over Confidence
A model's confidence or certainty does not establish factual correctness. Confidently expressed statements may still be hallucinated.

### 3.2 Claim-Level Verification
Analyze individual factual claims rather than treating an entire response as one indivisible unit.

### 3.3 Multi-Signal Detection
Use complementary detection mechanisms. No individual signal should automatically determine factual correctness.

### 3.4 Explainability
Every detection result should provide an understandable explanation supported by evidence and signal-level outputs.

### 3.5 Uncertainty Awareness
The system must distinguish between:
- Supported claims.
- Contradicted claims.
- Claims with insufficient evidence.
- Claims with conflicting evidence.
- Non-factual statements and opinions.

Do not force uncertain claims into a true/false classification.

### 3.6 Reproducibility
Record model versions, prompts, datasets, parameters, random seeds, retrieval configurations, and evaluation procedures.

### 3.7 Modular Architecture
Every detection module must be independently testable, replaceable, and measurable.

### 3.8 Responsible Verification
Never fabricate sources, citations, quotations, evidence, or experimental results.

## 4. HARMONIQ Architecture

The framework consists of four primary detection modules integrated through a centralized decision-fusion mechanism.

### 4.1 Overall Workflow

```text
                 USER QUERY
                      |
                      v
              LARGE LANGUAGE MODEL
                      |
                      v
              GENERATED RESPONSE
                      |
                      v
              CLAIM DECOMPOSITION
                      |
                      v
          HYBRID DETECTION ENGINE
                      |
       +--------------+--------------+
       |              |              |
       v              v              v
  CONFIDENCE      RETRIEVAL       SEMANTIC
   ANALYSIS      VERIFICATION   CONSISTENCY
       |              |              |
       +--------------+--------------+
                      |
                      v
              SELF-VERIFICATION
                      |
                      v
               DECISION FUSION
                      |
                      v
            CLAIM-LEVEL CLASSIFICATION
                      |
                      v
           RESPONSE-LEVEL AGGREGATION
                      |
                      v
             HALLUCINATION SCORE
                      |
                      v
           EVIDENCE & EXPLANATION
                      |
                      v
        RELIABLE RESPONSE / WARNING
```

### 4.2 Module One: Confidence Analysis

**Purpose:** Evaluate uncertainty and confidence-related indicators associated with generated factual claims.

Potential techniques:

- Token probability analysis.
- Log probability analysis.
- Predictive uncertainty.
- Entropy-based uncertainty estimation.
- Confidence calibration.
- Sampling-based consistency.
- Model-reported confidence as a separately evaluated proxy.

The implementation must distinguish between actual model probabilities and confidence estimates obtained through prompts.

If the model API does not expose token probabilities, explicitly document the alternative uncertainty-estimation method.

**Expected output:** A normalized confidence-related risk signal.

### 4.3 Module Two: Retrieval-Based Verification

**Purpose:** Retrieve external evidence to assess whether generated factual claims are supported or contradicted.

Potential techniques:

- Search-engine retrieval.
- Wikipedia retrieval.
- Knowledge-base lookup.
- Document retrieval.
- Vector similarity search.
- Dense retrieval.
- BM25 retrieval.
- RAG-based evidence collection.

The module should:

1. Receive an extracted factual claim.
2. Construct an appropriate retrieval query.
3. Retrieve relevant documents or passages.
4. Rank the evidence.
5. Evaluate evidence relevance and authority.
6. Determine whether evidence supports, contradicts, or does not address the claim.

For every evidence item, preserve source metadata whenever available:

- Source title.
- URL or persistent identifier.
- Publisher.
- Publication date.
- Retrieval timestamp.
- Relevant text passage.
- Relevance score.
- Claim associated with the evidence.

Do not assume that the absence of supporting evidence means a claim is false.

**Expected output:** An evidence-based verification signal.

### 4.4 Module Three: Semantic Consistency Analysis

**Purpose:** Evaluate semantic relationships between generated claims and available reference evidence or supplied context.

Potential techniques:

- Sentence embeddings.
- Transformer-based embeddings.
- Cosine similarity.
- Natural Language Inference (NLI).
- Semantic entailment.
- Contradiction detection.
- Contextual similarity analysis.

The module should evaluate whether the evidence semantically supports, contradicts, or is unrelated to a particular claim.

Important distinction:

Semantic similarity is not equivalent to factual correctness. Two sentences can be semantically similar while containing contradictory information.

**Expected output:** A semantic consistency or contradiction signal.

### 4.5 Module Four: Self-Verification

**Purpose:** Use an independent verification process to assess generated factual claims.

Potential techniques:

- LLM-based fact checking.
- Independent verification prompts.
- Multi-sample consistency checking.
- Question decomposition.
- Claim-specific verification.
- Alternative reasoning paths.
- Structured verification outputs.

The verification process should ideally use a different prompt, sample, or model configuration from the original generation process.

However, self-verification is not an independent source of truth. A model may repeat its original mistakes.

**Expected output:** A model-based verification signal with an uncertainty assessment.

## 5. Claim Decomposition

Claim decomposition is an essential preprocessing stage.

The framework should convert a generated response into atomic factual statements.

For example:

**Original Response:**

"Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh. Bangladesh became independent in 1971. The country has 64 administrative districts."

**Extracted Claims:**

1. Dhaka is the capital of Bangladesh.
2. Bangladesh became independent in 1971.
3. Bangladesh has 64 administrative districts.

Each claim should be independently evaluated by the detection modules.

### Claim Extraction Requirements

- Preserve the original meaning.
- Retain important qualifiers.
- Preserve dates, numbers, entities, and locations.
- Avoid introducing new factual information.
- Separate compound claims when independently verifiable.
- Maintain a mapping to the original response.
- Support claim-level hallucination localization.

## 6. Decision-Fusion Mechanism

Decision fusion is the central integration component of HARMONIQ.

It combines the outputs of the four detection modules to estimate the hallucination risk associated with each claim.

### 6.1 Initial Mathematical Model

A weighted fusion model may be investigated:

H = w_c C + w_r R + w_s S + w_v V

Where:

- H = Overall hallucination-risk score.
- C = Confidence-related risk signal.
- R = Retrieval-based verification risk signal.
- S = Semantic inconsistency risk signal.
- V = Self-verification risk signal.
- w_c = Confidence weight.
- w_r = Retrieval weight.
- w_s = Semantic weight.
- w_v = Self-verification weight.

The weights should be determined through validation experiments or an appropriate learning procedure.

### 6.2 Fusion Requirements

- Normalize signal directions and scales.
- Document each signal's meaning.
- Handle unavailable signals explicitly.
- Avoid silently replacing missing values with zero.
- Evaluate different weighting strategies.
- Compare weighted fusion with individual methods.
- Avoid selecting thresholds using test data.
- Evaluate calibration if scores are interpreted probabilistically.

### 6.3 Alternative Fusion Approaches

Potential approaches for experimentation:

- Weighted averaging.
- Rule-based fusion.
- Logistic regression.
- Random forest.
- Gradient boosting.
- Learned meta-classification.
- Confidence-aware dynamic weighting.

Do not assume a more complex fusion model is necessarily better.

## 7. Classification and Output Labels

HARMONIQ should support the following claim-level labels:

| Label | Description |
|---|---|
| Supported | Reliable evidence supports the claim. |
| Contradicted | Reliable evidence conflicts with the claim. |
| Insufficient Evidence | Available evidence cannot establish or refute the claim. |
| Conflicting Evidence | Credible sources disagree or provide materially inconsistent information. |
| Non-Factual / Opinion | The statement is not a directly verifiable factual claim. |

Define how these categories map to binary hallucination labels before calculating binary classification metrics.

A claim without evidence should not automatically be classified as false.

## 10. Experimental Evaluation

HARMONIQ must be evaluated against individual detection approaches.

### 10.1 Required Baselines

1. Confidence-only detection.
2. Retrieval-only verification.
3. Semantic-consistency-only detection.
4. Self-verification-only detection.
5. Full HARMONIQ hybrid framework.

Where suitable, compare against established methods from the literature.

### 10.2 Evaluation Metrics

Consider the following metrics:

**Classification Metrics**
- Accuracy.
- Precision.
- Recall.
- F1-score.
- ROC-AUC.
- PR-AUC.

**Error Metrics**
- False-positive rate.
- False-negative rate.
- Hallucination detection rate.

**Claim-Level Metrics**
- Claim localization precision.
- Claim localization recall.
- Claim localization F1.

**Calibration Metrics**
- Brier score.
- Expected Calibration Error.

**Efficiency Metrics**
- Average inference latency.
- Token consumption.
- Number of LLM calls.
- Retrieval latency.
- Computational cost.
- Memory consumption.

Select metrics based on the dataset and evaluation task. Report class distributions and confidence intervals where feasible.

## 12. Error Analysis

Investigate the following potential failure categories:

- Incorrect claim decomposition.
- Missing factual qualifiers.
- Retrieval failures.
- Irrelevant retrieved evidence.
- Outdated sources.
- Contradictory sources.
- Semantic similarity without factual entailment.
- Incorrect self-verification.
- Numerical hallucinations.
- Temporal factual errors.
- Entity confusion.
- Multi-hop reasoning errors.
- Ambiguous claims.
- Subjective statements.
- Domain-specific hallucinations.
- Language-dependent performance differences.

Document representative examples and explain why the framework succeeded or failed.

## 13. Explainability Requirements

For every detected hallucination, the system should provide:

1. Original claim text.
2. Claim-level classification.
3. Hallucination-risk score.
4. Confidence-related signal.
5. Retrieval result.
6. Semantic consistency result.
7. Self-verification result.
8. Supporting or contradictory evidence.
9. Source information.
10. Concise explanation of the final decision.

## 19. Scope and Limitations

The initial research scope is text-based hallucination detection in LLM-generated responses.

Do not automatically expand the project into:

- Image hallucination detection.
- Audio hallucination detection.
- Video hallucination detection.
- Multimodal factuality assessment.
- Automated response correction.
- Real-time production monitoring.
- Domain-specific clinical or legal decision systems.

These can be considered future extensions.

HARMONIQ must not be described as:

- A guarantee of factual correctness.
- A replacement for expert review.
- A universal hallucination detector.
- A system proven to work across every language and domain.
- A source of truth independent of evidence quality.

Clearly document limitations, dataset dependencies, model biases, retrieval weaknesses, and uncertainty.
